Feature reference

Rytm Mobile

What the app does, tab by tab, and what runs on your computer for Split.

Overview

signal path

Rytm Mobile has three tabs, Rytm, Chop and Split, on a bottom bar drawn as a patch bay: a jack for each tab, with a cable plugged into the one you're on.

A jack lights up when its tab has something going: the Rytm is connected, a sample is loaded, or a split is done.

The app keeps your sample and the USB link when you rotate or fold the phone.

One job runs at a time. The status line under the title says what's happening, with a 16‑step progress bar; errors turn it red.

Rytm

+Drive over USB-MIDI

Connect

Patch the Rytm into the phone with a USB‑C OTG cable, set its USB mode to USB MIDI, and tap Patch in.

The app looks for a MIDI device whose name says Elektron or Rytm, tries each pair of its ports, and keeps the first that answers. If no Elektron device shows up, it tries any USB MIDI device.

Once connected, the panel shows the Rytm's name and the OS version it reports.

Android can open Rytm Mobile when you plug in an Elektron USB device, and the app connects as it opens. While it's open, plugging the Rytm in connects it, and unplugging drops the link at once.

If the link goes stale (sleep, a cable wiggle, folding the phone) and the Rytm stops answering, the app reconnects once and repeats what you asked.

Browse +Drive

Tap through folders with the path at the top, .. to go up, and Refresh. Folders come first, then samples, by name.

Each sample shows its length in seconds.

Make a folder from the New folder field. Rytm names use letters, digits, spaces, _ and -, up to 24 characters; anything else becomes _.

Delete a sample with the x button. The app asks first: "It goes from the Rytm's +Drive. Samples already loaded into RAM stay."

Folders can't be deleted or renamed from the app, and samples can't be renamed.

Send

Send puts your chop in the folder that's open on the Rytm tab, under its Name on Rytm.

Format
48 kHz, 16‑bit mono, the Rytm's native format.
Protocol
Elektron's SysEx file‑transfer protocol, ported from Elektroid.
Pace
8,192‑byte blocks with a 50 ms rest between them, as Elektroid does.
When it's done
The status line shows the sample's length and how long the send took.

The Rytm gets up to 20 seconds to make room for a new file.

Checked against Elektroid

The repository builds Elektroid's own C message code, and a unit test checks the app's SysEx against it byte for byte.

Another test decodes a real ping reply from an Analog Rytm MK1.

Log

Show log lists what the app tried and what the Rytm answered while connecting, then one line per message during transfers. It keeps the last 400 lines, and Copy puts it on the clipboard.

Chop

trim and send

Open

Open any audio or video file on the phone, or share one to Rytm Mobile from another app.

MP3, WAV (16, 24 and 32-bit), M4A and FLAC open, and so do screen recordings and voice memos. Beyond that it depends on the phone's own decoders.

A video gives up its first sound track. Stereo and multichannel sound is mixed down to mono.

Convert

Anything that isn't 48 kHz is resampled with a Kaiser-windowed sinc filter, 64 zero crossings each side, with the cutoff below the lower Nyquist so downsampling doesn't alias.

The 16‑bit conversion adds TPDF dither.

Trim

Drag the start and end handles on the waveform, or tap to move the nearest one.

Start, End, Length and Size readouts follow along; size counts 96 KB per second of sound.

Shape

Normalize, on by default, sets the selection's peak to ‑0.3 dBFS.

Fades, on by default, add a 2 ms fade at each end so the cut points don't click.

Play auditions the trimmed clip with those settings; Stop stops it.

Name, send, save, share

Name on Rytm starts as the file's name, cleaned to Rytm rules.

Send goes to the folder open on the Rytm tab. Rytm not connected? Chop says so and offers Go to Rytm.

Save writes the chop to Music/Rytm Mobile/Chops as a 48 kHz 16‑bit mono WAV, the same audio Send puts on the Rytm. Saving the same name again doesn't overwrite the earlier one.

Share sends a WAV copy through Android's share sheet.

Saving needs no storage permission: files go through Android's MediaStore.

Record

mic or USB audio

Record straight into the phone, from its own microphone or from a USB audio input.

Then the recording goes through the whole process like any other sound: chop it, split it, save it, or send it to the Rytm.

Split

stems, on your computer

Split separates a track into stems. The splitting runs on your own Mac or Linux computer, which the phone reaches on your home Wi‑Fi or over Tailscale.

Modes

ModeStemsModel
6 stems (Full band)drums, bass, guitar, piano, other, vocals.BS‑RoFormer SW, or HTDemucs 6‑stem on computers that can't run it.
4 stems, fast (Fast)drums, bass, other, vocals.HTDemucs.
Vocalsvocal and instrumental.Mel‑Band RoFormer, or BS‑RoFormer ViperX 1297.
Drum kitthe drum stem split into kit pieces (the model lists kick, snare, toms, hats, ride and crash), plus the full‑band stems, with the whole drum stem kept as drums_full.MDX23C DrumSep, run on the drum stem of the 6‑stem split.

The app only offers the split modes your computer can run.

How a split runs

  • Pick a track or a video, screen recordings too. The phone uploads it with a progress bar.
  • The computer pulls out the sound with ffmpeg; any video is dropped.
  • Splits run one at a time. The app shows how many are queued ahead, then a moving signal path while the computer works.
  • Each stem comes back two ways: a Rytm‑ready 48 kHz mono WAV and a full-quality stereo FLAC.

Close the app mid-split and Resume last job picks it up again.

If the computer restarts during a split, that split is marked failed, with a note to split it again. Finished splits survive restarts.

Each stem

Every stem has Play, Chop, Save and Share. Chop opens it in the Chop tab, ready to trim and send.

Save to the phone

Full quality
Stereo FLAC at 44.1 kHz, exactly as the computer split it. Best for a DAW. This is the default.
Rytm-ready
48 kHz mono WAV, the same file Send puts on the Rytm.
Folders
Stems go to Music/Rytm Mobile/<track> (<mode>), one folder per split and mode, so two modes of one song don't overwrite each other. Saving the same split again replaces what the app saved before.
Save all
Save all saves every stem in one go. A stem the computer has no FLAC for is saved as its 48 kHz WAV, and the app names it.
Zip
The computer packs every stem into one zip, saved to Download/Rytm Mobile. A stem with no file in that format is left out and listed in MISSING.txt inside the zip.
Share
Share any stem through Android's share sheet.

A split made before full-quality saving existed only has the WAV; the app switches to Rytm‑ready and says so.

Send all to the Rytm

With the Rytm connected, Send all makes a folder named after the track (its first 12 characters) in the folder open on the Rytm tab and sends every stem there, each normalized and faded. Then it opens that folder.

History

History lists every split on your computer, newest first, with its mode, how many stems came out and how long ago. Six show at first; Show all lists the rest.

Tap a split to reopen it and play, chop, save or send its stems again.

Delete removes a split from the computer; copies saved on the phone stay. A split that is still queued or running can't be deleted.

Your computer

the stem server

Pairing

Pair once with the installer's code, or type the server and token in. The app saves them only after the computer has answered.

Rytm Mobile opens on its Split tab, fills in the server and token, and checks that the computer answers.

Open Split later and it reconnects to the saved computer by itself, so History and your last split are there.

On the computer

  • Every request carries the token, and the server refuses to start without one.
  • The server keeps one model in memory and works through splits in order.
  • The stem server cuts input to the first 30 minutes and refuses uploads over 2,048 MB.
  • The server won't start a new split when its disk has less than 2 GB free.
  • On an Intel Mac the 6‑stem split uses HTDemucs 6‑stem instead of BS‑RoFormer SW, because the newer model needs a newer PyTorch than Intel Macs can run.
  • On Linux with an NVIDIA card, the installer sets up the GPU build by itself. NVIDIA GPUs haven't been tested.

Its two commands

pair shows the code again, using the Tailscale address when Tailscale is on the computer and the home network address otherwise.

Re-running the installer updates the server and keeps your token, splits and models. uninstall moves everything aside and deletes nothing.

Step-by-step setup is on the download page.

Updates

SHA-256 checked

The app reads this site's version.json when it starts and when you come back to it, at most every 6 hours.

A newer version shows an Update banner with its version and notes.

Update downloads the new APK and checks its SHA‑256 against version.json. If it doesn't match, the file is deleted and nothing is installed.

A good file goes to Android's own installer, so you confirm the install. Android installs it over your copy only if it's signed with the same key.

The first time, Android asks you to let Rytm Mobile install apps; the app opens that setting for you.

If the check fails, for example when you're offline, nothing pops up.

Support

never locked

Rytm Mobile is free software under the GPL‑3.0, and every feature works for everyone, whether you support it or not.

Now and then, never in your first session, a small card asks you to support it. Later or x closes it for this session; I've supported it hides it for good. The Rytm tab also has a Support button any time.

Good to know

  • It only sends. It lists, sends, makes folders and deletes samples on +Drive; it can't copy samples back off the Rytm.
  • Folders stay put. Folders can't be deleted or renamed from the app, and samples can't be renamed.
  • Plain HTTP at home. The phone talks to the stem server over plain HTTP, with the token on every request. Use it on your own Wi‑Fi or over Tailscale.
  • Phone. Android 10 or newer, on a phone with USB host and MIDI support.
  • Permissions: network access, and permission to install its own updates.

Under the hood

The stem server is a small FastAPI program. Its endpoints:

  • POST /jobs
  • GET /jobs
  • GET /jobs/{id}
  • GET /jobs/{id}/stems/{name}
  • GET /jobs/{id}/zip
  • DELETE /jobs/{id}
  • GET /models

Facts on this page come from the app's own source code and tests, the stem server and its installer, and the README.

The transfer protocol is ported from Elektroid (GPL‑3.0), so Rytm Mobile is GPL‑3.0 too.

Stems use BS‑RoFormer SW, Mel‑Band RoFormer, BS‑RoFormer ViperX, HTDemucs and MDX23C DrumSep through python-audio-separator. The models belong to their authors.
